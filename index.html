Contradictions across the literature

1. Do ensembles really win?

Zhao (2025) and Al Mamun et al. (2026) find clear ensemble advantages (XGBoost AUC 0.992 vs. LR 0.906; LightGBM 0.762 vs. LR 0.708). In contrast, Zhang (2026) reports LR as the best model (AUC 0.764 vs. GBM 0.762), and Khalid (2025) finds LR marginally ahead in AUC (0.790 vs. 0.780) with higher recall.

2. Accuracy vs. interpretability

Zhao (2025) and Dong et al. (2026) prioritise predictive metrics. Firdavs & Wang (2026) argue accuracy alone cannot justify model choice under Basel IRB. Dong et al. and Al Mamun et al. rely on SHAP, while Khalid (2025) and Firdavs & Wang propose hybrid LR + ML deployment.

3. Methods drive the results

SMOTE oversampling in Zhao (2025) coincides with AUCs above 0.99, whereas Al Mamun et al. (2026) found SMOTE gave lower validation AUC (0.749) than class reweighting (0.762) and removed post-origination leakage variables. Small datasets (1,000–30,000 rows) keep LR competitive.

Synthesis

Polished with Paperpal

Across six recent studies, ensemble models such as XGBoost, LightGBM and Random Forest generally outperformed logistic regression in credit default prediction, with the largest gains observed on large datasets such as LendingClub (Al Mamun et al., 2026; Zhao, 2025). However, the evidence is not uniform: logistic regression achieved the highest test AUC on the UCI Taiwan dataset (Zhang, 2026), while the models performed almost equally on the German Credit dataset, where logistic regression detected more defaulters (Khalid, 2025). Methodological differences appear to account for much of this variation: oversampling with SMOTE produced very high AUC values (Zhao, 2025), whereas a leakage-aware pipeline without synthetic data yielded more realistic results (Al Mamun et al., 2026). In addition, regulatory requirements such as Basel IRB continue to favour transparent models, prompting several authors to suggest hybrid approaches that combine ensemble accuracy with logistic regression or SHAP-based explanations (Firdavs & Wang, 2026; Dong et al., 2026).

Research gaps relevant to Business Informatics

Gap 1 · Data leakage and synthetic sampling in ML pipelines

Production scoring engines need clean, pre-origination features. Inflated benchmark scores from SMOTE or leaked repayment variables fail in live lending systems (Zhao, 2025; Al Mamun et al., 2026).

Gap 2 · Operationalising XAI for regulators

Global SHAP rankings do not yet provide the loan-level, auditable explanations required under Basel IRB and adverse-action rules (Firdavs & Wang, 2026; Al Mamun et al., 2026; Khalid, 2025).

Gap 3 · Generalisation, drift and business loss

Studies rely on single public datasets and symmetric metrics like AUC, ignoring out-of-time drift and the higher cost of missed defaulters (Zhang, 2026; Al Mamun et al., 2026; Khalid, 2025).
